Logowanie korzysta z openid, userinfo.email i userinfo.profile. Odczytujemy identyfikator Google, imię i adres e-mail, aby utworzyć lub rozpoznać konto Klyo. W bazie zapisujemy identyfikator Google, imię i e-mail. Nie otrzymujemy hasła do konta Google.

Odczytujemy adres e-mail połączonego konta i zapisujemy token odświeżający oraz informacje o połączeniu. Integracja Gmail nie odczytuje skrzynki ani historii wiadomości. Treść i odbiorcy utworzonych ofert oraz historia ich wysyłki są przechowywani jako dane CRM.

Zakres https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.calendarlist.readonly służy do wyświetlenia listy dostępnych kalendarzy i wyboru jednego z nich. Zakres https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events pozwala odczytywać wydarzenia z wybranego kalendarza oraz tworzyć lub aktualizować spotkania po jawnym poleceniu użytkownika. Dostęp openid i email pozwala rozpoznać połączone konto. Odczytywane dane obejmują identyfikatory i nazwy kalendarzy, uprawnienia do zapisu oraz identyfikatory, tytuły, opisy, lokalizacje, terminy i odnośniki wydarzeń.

W bazie zapisujemy zaszyfrowane tokeny dostępu i odświeżania, identyfikator i adres konta, wybór kalendarza oraz metadane powiązań i publikacji. Wydarzenia zewnętrzne odczytujemy do wyświetlenia; nie archiwizujemy automatycznie całego kalendarza. Gdy jawnie skopiujesz wydarzenie do CRM, jego treść zostaje zapisana jako wydarzenie CRM. Publikowanie i aktualizowanie spotkania przekazuje do Google jego tytuł, opis, lokalizację i terminy. Integracja nie zaprasza uczestników.

Połączenie z kalendarzem jest prywatne dla Twojego konta w aktywnej przestrzeni Team lub Personal. Dopiero wydarzenie jawnie skopiowane do CRM jest dostępne zgodnie z uprawnieniami tej przestrzeni.

Token dostępu pobieramy w celu realizacji żądania. Wgrywane i pobierane pliki przechodzą przez serwer Klyo; powiązanie CRM przechowuje metadane, a dokument źródłowy pozostaje w Dropboxie. 5. Google data — access, use and Limited Use

Klyo is a CRM for managing contacts, companies, sales opportunities, tasks and PDF quotes. Google integrations are optional. Signing in with Google does not grant Gmail, Calendar or Drive access. Each integration requests its permissions when you choose to connect it.

5.1. Google sign-in and Gmail

Sign-in uses openid, userinfo.email and userinfo.profile. We read your Google account identifier, name and email address to create or identify your Klyo account, and store that identifier, name and email address. We do not receive your Google password.

If an administrator separately connects Gmail, https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send lets Klyo send messages and selected attachments to your chosen recipients after approval in Klyo. We read the connected account's email address and store a refresh token and connection information. The Gmail integration does not read your inbox or message history. The content and recipients of offers you create and their sending history are stored as CRM data.

5.2. Google Calendar

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.calendarlist.readonly lists accessible calendars so you can select one. https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events lets Klyo read the selected calendar's events and create or update appointments when you explicitly request it. openid and email identify the connected account. Data accessed includes calendar identifiers and names, write permissions, and event identifiers, titles, descriptions, locations, dates, times and links.

We store encrypted access and refresh tokens, the account identifier and email address, your calendar selection, and linking/publication metadata. External events are read for display; we do not automatically archive your entire calendar. When you explicitly copy an external event to CRM, its contents are stored as a CRM event. Publishing or updating an appointment sends its title, description, location and dates/times to Google. The integration does not invite attendees.

A calendar connection is private to your account in the active Team or Personal space. Only events you explicitly copy to CRM become accessible under that workspace's permissions.

5.3. Google Drive

The current integration requests https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive. This is broad permission over the connected Google account's Drive files, not a Google-enforced permission limited to one folder. It supports browsing existing folders, selecting documents, and uploading and downloading files. During setup the owner selects a CRM folder; Klyo then restricts CRM file operations in that workspace to the selected folder and its descendants.

We access connected-account information, folder identifiers, names and hierarchy, and document metadata including type, size and modification time. File contents pass through the Klyo server when you request an upload or download. The database stores encrypted tokens, connection and selected-folder information, and metadata linking files to CRM records. This integration does not create a permanent archive of Drive file contents in the Klyo database.

In Team, the owner configures the connection and authorized workspace members access linked documents through the connected account according to their roles. Personal uses a separate, private connection. Select a folder intended for that workspace. Removing a file link from Klyo does not delete the original Google Drive file.

In Files → Google Drive → Disconnect, the owner removes the connection, tokens and that integration's file links from Klyo; original Drive files remain. Disconnecting Gmail removes its stored token and sending configuration while keeping created offers and CRM history.

You can also revoke Klyo's access from your Google Account's third-party connections. Revocation prevents further authorized access but does not itself delete data already copied to CRM, metadata or downloaded files. To remove remaining Klyo data, delete the relevant records or request deletion at clash.09xx@gmail.com. Account data and CRM copies follow section 9's retention policy. Backups and documents held outside Klyo follow their providers' applicable procedures; disconnecting does not immediately erase those copies.

5A. Optional Dropbox integration

Dropbox is an optional connection for managing CRM documents. A workspace owner, or a user in their Personal space, connects an account through OAuth, selects an existing CRM folder, and can browse its subfolders, link files to CRM records, upload new files and download linked documents.

The scopes files.metadata.read, files.content.read and files.content.write permit metadata access, downloads and uploads. Full Dropbox authorization covers the Dropbox account rather than a single folder. During setup we read folders needed to select the location; after selection, Klyo restricts document operations to that folder and its subfolders.

We store the account and connection identifiers, encrypted refresh token, selected folder and linked-file metadata: identifier, name, size and CRM record association. We obtain an access token to perform requests. Uploads and downloads pass through the Klyo server; CRM links store metadata while source documents remain in Dropbox. The transfer limit is 20 MB per file.

Files linked in Team space are available to members of that workspace according to their permissions. Personal space uses a separate connection. Removing a CRM link does not delete the Dropbox original. Disconnecting through Files → Dropbox → Disconnect deletes the stored connection, token and its file links. Data remaining in CRM records and previously downloaded copies follow section 9. You can also revoke access in Dropbox's connected-app settings; revocation does not erase copies already downloaded or data already recorded in CRM. Processing by Dropbox is governed by its own privacy policy.

Calendar and Drive connection tokens are additionally encrypted by the application. Infrastructure access is restricted to operating the service.
